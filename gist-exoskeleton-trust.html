Trust-Aware Human-Exoskeleton Interaction

Overview 

Research Assistant, Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology (GIST):
Summer 2024 – Present, Advisor: Prof. Pilwon Hur

As a Research Assistant, and now as an M.Sc. student in Mechanical and Robotics Engineering at GIST, I develop trust-aware control strategies for physically coupled human-exoskeleton interaction. I built a Trust-POMDP (Partially Observable Markov Decision Process) algorithm that estimates and adapts to a human operator's trust in real time, and this has grown into ongoing research on real-time state-anxiety estimation for adaptive wearable exoskeleton control.

Summary

Physically coupled human-robot interaction — like a person walking with a powered exoskeleton — only works if the person trusts the device enough to relax into it. My research asks how a wearable robot can estimate that trust (and the anxiety that erodes it) in real time, and adapt its assistance accordingly.

At GIST, under Prof. Pilwon Hur, I developed a Trust-POMDP (Partially Observable Markov Decision Process) algorithm that treats a human operator's trust as a hidden state to be estimated from observable behavior during human-exoskeleton interaction. This is the foundation for my ongoing M.Sc. work on real-time state-anxiety estimation for adaptive wearable exoskeleton control.

Publications

Peer-Reviewed: Mahta Akhyani, Pilwon Hur, “Real-Time State Anxiety Estimation for Adaptive Wearable Exoskeleton Control,” Proc. 41st ICROS Annual Conference (ICROS 2026), Daegu, Korea, pp. 199–200, Jul. 2026.

Under Review: Kwonseung Cho, Mahta Akhyani, Pilwon Hur, “Trust in Physically Coupled Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI): A Review and Measurement Framework for Direct Body-Robot Coupling,” ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction — major revision submitted.

This project is ongoing graduate research — a fuller write-up and photos are coming as the work progresses.
